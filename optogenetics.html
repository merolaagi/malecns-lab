Switch on one cell type with light

In real flies, a light-gated channel is put into one genetically targeted cell type and switched on with light. Several of these experiments have well-known results: backward walking, forward walking, turning, escape. Here the same cell type is driven in the lab's model and the legs are scored against what real flies do. Matching these is the minimum before the model could plan experiments on a real fly.

Measured: the cells, their chemical synapses and transmitter predictions. Assumed: identical simple spiking cells, synapse strength from synapse counts, glutamate as inhibitory, light as a constant current, an engineered leg readout that turns away from the side whose legs are more active and cannot walk backwards, and the verdict thresholds. Missing: electrical synapses, neuromodulation, muscles and body mechanics.

Physics body in closed loop: the circuit driving NeuroMechFly

NeuroMechFly v2 (flygym, MuJoCo) is driven by the circuit every millisecond, and the body feeds back. Motor neurons offset each joint by the balance of opposing muscles. Each leg's stepping rhythm is switched on, sped up and turned forward or backward by the circuit: its motor drive sets amplitude and frequency, and coxa retractors against protractors set the direction. Foot load and joint movement drive the leg's sensory neurons. The jump-muscle motor neurons drive a twitching muscle on the middle legs. The circuit builder adds every selected cell type's partners on the other side, so neither side is favoured by extraction. Turning cells are lit while the fly walks under its own forward command (P9 on both sides); everything else is lit on a standing fly.

Why doesn't the fly walk? A diagnosis

Three assumptions tested against each other on the left-right complete circuit, without tuning: the light strength, the sign of glutamate, and the step-direction rule for the front legs. Then a fourth question the first three raised: when the circuit does make every leg step forward, why doesn't the body go anywhere?

Coordinating the legs: adding the interneurons that link them

The coordination circuit adds each leg's premotor interneurons, interneurons that reach two or more leg segments, and P9's route to the front legs. It also measures how strongly each leg's premotor pool reaches every other leg's; that leg-link matrix can set the coupling between leg rhythms instead of a uniform constant. The question: does the drive now reach every leg evenly, and does the fly walk?
