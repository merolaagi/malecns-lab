Rewiring preserves each node’s incoming/outgoing degree and actual self-loops. Initialization has matched per-node weight norms. It does not preserve cell-type or spatial wiring. The dense baseline has approximately the two-state model’s parameter count; computation and state capacity differ.

Trainable eye. The two eye models put a photoreceptor stage in front of the measured graph: adaptation y = a·y + (1−a)·x, output g·(x − k·y) with shared a, k, g, then a learned column-to-column projection into L1/L2/L3. The retinotopic version may only mix each column with its six hex neighbours, like neural superposition; the unrestricted version may mix any columns. Both start as an exact identity, so untrained they equal the two-state measured graph. “Eye reset” re-evaluates the trained network with the eye returned to identity, measuring how much the rest of the network came to rely on it.

What is measured, what is learned, and what remains untested?

Measured: a radius-2 right optic-lobe patch containing L1/L2/L3, Mi1/Mi4/Mi9, C2/C3, Tm1/Tm2/Tm9. The real graph has 209 cells and 1,421 directed edges. T4/T5 and connections outside this patch are omitted.

Learned: existing-edge weights, a local state-mixing matrix and bias per node, input gains at L1/L2/L3, and an artificial readout pooling Tm1/Tm2/Tm9. Input bypasses photoreceptor physiology. Signs are unconstrained; synapse counts are not electrical weights. Local recurrence is an added dynamical rule.

Inside a node: sum incoming messages, mix the previous local state, add sensory drive and bias, apply tanh, then retain half the previous state. Two-state nodes have a learned 2×2 internal matrix. This is a tiny recurrent computational model, not a reconstruction of dendrites or ion channels.

Task: estimate 2D image-plane drift and classify a synthetic looming hazard. Backpropagation through 10 frames trains the entire network. The system does not yet reconstruct metric depth, control the fly, or learn abstract mathematics.
