The connectome as the backbone of a learning network

Until now the lab froze the wiring and trained a readout on top, and shuffled wiring did as well as measured. Here learning happens inside the wiring: the network has exactly the connectome's connections, the strengths are learned, and each cell keeps the sign its transmitter predicts. Three things are varied, one at a time: the learning rule (from reward and punishment only, up to backpropagation), the wiring (measured, shuffled, random), and the biology of the units.

Measured: which cells connect, synapse counts (starting strengths), transmitter predictions (signs), cell types, input counts (cell size), the dopamine neurons' inputs and targets. Assumed: the unit model, the number of update steps, how task inputs reach the input cells, every learning rule and rate, the tasks. Three seeds throughout. Backpropagation is the upper bound, not a claim about biology.

A second backbone: the leg motor circuit

Reward and punishment through the fly's own dopamine wiring

A harder test on the mushroom body, wired as measured (projection neurons → 4,064 Kenyon cells → 97 output neurons). The network is told only whether its action was right. The synapses onto the output neurons learn, as in the fly, and dopamine decides how. The question: does the measured dopamine wiring (340 dopamine neurons, each hearing some output neurons and teaching others) deliver that signal to the right places?
