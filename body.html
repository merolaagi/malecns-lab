Pose the fly, joint by joint

The full body at inspection size: six legs with four joints each, wings, head, antennae, proboscis, abdomen and halteres. Pose it by hand, or let the measured circuit drive the legs.

Only the legs have motor neurons behind them. Each leg's coxa, trochanter and tibia angle comes from the motor neurons innervating that joint's muscles, and the tarsus from the front legs' tarsal motor neurons; the arena's simulation supplies them. Head, antennae, proboscis, abdomen, wings and halteres have no motor neurons in this subset, so they move only from these controls. A fly's compound eyes cannot move at all. Segment proportions and the mesh are drawn by eye; there is no muscle model, load or inertia.
